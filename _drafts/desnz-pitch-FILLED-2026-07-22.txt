SUBJECT: Plug-in solar becomes legal in Britain on 27 August — regs made 16 July (SI 2026/848)

Hi [[FIRST_NAME]],

Quick, no-fluff heads-up in case it's useful for coverage.

The regulations that open a legal route for plug-in (balcony) solar were made
on 16 July 2026 and laid before Parliament on 17 July. They come into force on
27 August 2026 — so this is a dated, concrete change, not a consultation step.

The essentials, straight from the instrument:

  - Instrument: The Plugs and Sockets etc. (Safety) Regulations 1994 and
    Electricity Safety, Quality and Continuity Regulations 2002 (Amendment)
    Regulations 2026 — SI 2026 No. 848
  - Made 16 July 2026; laid before Parliament 17 July 2026
  - In force: 27 August 2026
  - What it does: PSSR reg 8 is amended so a notified body can approve a
    standard plug for a "plug-in microgenerator" where it fails BS 1363 only
    because BS 1363 restricts using a plug to connect a generating device.
    ESQCR reg 22(1)(c) is replaced so such a device must not be installed or
    operated unless it complies with the product specification.
  - Device definition: solar (direct conversion of sunlight); maximum rated
    AC output not exceeding 800W; connects to a low voltage consumer's
    installation by standard plug and socket; operates in parallel with the
    distributor's network; not designed to import electricity for storage
    (beyond control/auxiliary functions the specification permits).
  - The gatekeeper: Plug-in Solar Device Interim Product Specification,
    version 2, published 16 July 2026.
  - Extent: regs 1–2 England & Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland;
    reg 3 (ESQCR) England & Wales and Scotland.
  - Primary source: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/848/made

The angle we think is under-covered: the in-force date is 27 August, but
compliance is judged against Interim Product Specification v2 — and we are not
aware of any product yet certified to it. So there is a real gap between "legal
from 27 August" and "buyable, certified kit on a shelf". Anyone writing "legal
now" or implying today's imported kits qualify is getting ahead of the document.

I run BalconySolarHub, an independent UK guide that has tracked this since the
consultation opened. Happy to share our plain-English breakdown, or to be quoted
on what it means for renters and flat-dwellers, and on the 800W hardwired-vs-DIY
distinction. Not selling anything — just close to the detail.
