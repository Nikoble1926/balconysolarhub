Best UK balcony solar kits — 2026 honest picks

New UK rules — 27 August 2026: SI 2026 No. 848 (made 16 July 2026) creates a legal plug-in route for a solar device with a maximum rated AC output not exceeding 800W that complies with the Plug-in Solar Device Interim Product Specification v2. We are not aware of any product yet certified to that specification. Before buying for the plug-in route, check the ENA Type Test Register yourself — it is public and needs no login. Filter Type to “Plug-in Solar”. Read on 27 August 2026 at 19:19 it listed 22 devices: eight carried the status “Compliant” — seven from UKSOL Ltd and one from Perlight — and the other fourteen, including EcoFlow, Octopus Energy, FoxESS, City Plumbing Supplies, InstaGroup, TSUNESS, Segen, Modular and Anker, carried “Non-compliant”. There is no third-party certification against the specification itself, and IPS v2 §7 says a device merely submitted for registration does not count. Statuses change — check the register rather than trusting this page. What the law says →

Five options, one strict rule: if UK stock or compliance is unclear, we say “watch” instead of hiding it.

Quick answer: For UK buyers in mid-2026 the most mainstream balcony solar option is EcoFlow STREAM (broadest UK availability, ~£700–£1,000 with a small panel). For budget / minimal-fuss installs, an APsystems EZ1-M (~£120) or Hoymiles HMS-800 (~£107) microinverter paired with a single 400–500W panel is the cheapest compliant 800W setup on the hardwired route. Anker SOLIX Solarbank 2 and Zendure SolarFlow 800 add storage but UK Amazon stock is currently limited — check before ordering. Note that Interim Product Specification v2 excludes “Plug-in battery systems” and “Plug-in solar PV devices integrated with battery systems” from its scope (§1), so the STREAM, the Solarbank and the SolarFlow sit outside the plug-in route opening on 27 August 2026 — they stay what they are, hardwired systems with an electrician and a G98 notification. Whether a complete kit built around either microinverter would meet the specification depends on the whole device as defined in §3.10, which we have not established for any specific kit.

What about “wait for legalisation”?

That’s a defensible choice, but be clear about what you would be waiting for. The plug-in route opens on 27 August 2026 (SI 2026 No. 848), and it applies to a device certified to the Interim Product Specification v2. We are not aware of any product yet certified to it, including the five on this page. Waiting for the date is not the same as waiting for a compliant product — the question to put to a retailer is whether the device meets IPS v2, not when the rules change. Meanwhile the hardwired route with a CPS-registered electrician and a free G98 notification is available before 27 August 2026 and stays available after it, in a high-rate region (most of England & Wales is on the ~27p/kWh cap) at the same savings.
